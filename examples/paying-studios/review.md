# The partner's copy

**Meeting question:** Is the editor returning for a second cut because the first one worked?

### With the ledger

> Six of eight workspaces had premium features turned on (S2, W01–W08). The pilot had six external studios: A used two workspaces and W08 was internal (S1). Three studios had positive net receipts: A €120, B €80 and E €150. C’s €100 was refunded. That makes €350 in net receipts and 3/6 studios, or 50%, through 17 November (S3, L1–L5). Two first cuts were accepted with zero recorded founder delivery minutes; B’s took 22 founder minutes (S4, A/B/E). Acquisition effort and second-cut retention are open.
>
> “Two studios delivered a first cut with no recorded delivery help. I’d like to understand what happened on the next one.”
>
> Which changes did B need? Can we see dated second-cut deliveries and net receipts for this cohort once each studio has had 30 days?

The reader can accept different wording. Check each denominator, the net treatment of C, and the difference between an accepted client cut and an account billed. Omitting D’s receipt is correct: the complete ledger has none. There is no evidence that D's reason for having a paid flag was a gift, an error or anything more specific.

### With S3 withheld

> The dashboard still reports six of eight flagged workspaces. The roster still resolves to six external studios. Net-paying studios, net receipts and customer-conversion rate are unknown. Please supply the dated settlement entries, including refunds, joined by studio ID. The delivery evidence survives independently: A and E had accepted first cuts with zero recorded founder delivery minutes; B required 22 (S4).

An answer of 0% on the second run fails just as surely as 50% or 75%. The missing ledger prevents the calculation. The list of editors who accepted cuts cannot substitute for a payment record.

### In a real partner meeting

Bring the founder the discrepancy privately. Give them the observation window and the exact account mapping, and let them correct an omission before the memo goes round. If the investment case depends on repeat use, agree on the next editorial milestone, the eligible studios and the records that will count before you review another period.
